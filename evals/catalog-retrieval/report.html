The briefs and their gold answers

Written in a director's voice, never the shelf's, so neither retriever wins on vocabulary. Authored before any run and never shown to one.

brief 01

id: 01
duration: 4.0
register: developer tool
aspect: 16x9

The release goes out in five steps, and we want the viewer to hold all five in their head at once before we drop them into the one that broke. So: lay the whole run out across the frame, let it read as a complete route from first step to last, then take everything away except the step where it stopped. The narrowing is the point. By the end there should be no doubt which step we are now talking about, and no leftover clutter from the other four. Nothing here is being typed, spoken, or photographed. It is a status picture.

gold best: pill-row-collapse, state-chip-rail
known wrong: caption-camera-follow, glyph-ring-assemble

pill-row-collapse collapses to the FIRST stage, while the brief narrows to the stage that failed. A selection that names it should say how it reassigns which stage survives. Naming it without that note is defensible, not disqualifying.

brief 02

id: 02
duration: 3.2
register: quiet editorial
aspect: 16x9

This is the proof moment. We have been talking about what the treatment does to a photograph, and now the viewer has to actually see the difference rather than take our word for it. Keep it calm and keep it honest: one picture, the untouched version and the treated version visible together, so the eye can compare them without cutting away or remembering a previous shot. No narration over this beat. No text. The comparison is the whole argument, and if the viewer has to hold two separate shots in memory to make it, we have failed.

gold best: grade-split-reveal
known wrong: halftone-dissolve, match-cut

grade-split-reveal's avoid_when restricts it to image and video media. The brief says photograph, so the constraint is satisfied. A run that rejects it on those grounds has misread its own catalog.

brief 03

id: 03
duration: 6.0
register: consumer app
aspect: 9x16

Show someone actually using the thing. A short back-and-forth: they ask something, they get an answer, they follow up. It should feel like watching over a shoulder, so the exchange arrives piece by piece rather than appearing all at once, and the two sides of the conversation need to be immediately distinguishable. Behind it, the app should not look frozen. Something should keep drifting or breathing under the exchange so the scene stays alive while the talk accumulates. Phone shape. Warm, casual, not corporate.

gold best: thread-message-stack
known wrong: persona-card-fan, rank-list-settle

Its avoid_when is that frame zero cannot be empty, since it opens on three settled bubbles. The brief never requires an empty first frame, so this is not a blocker here.

brief 04

id: 04
duration: 3.5
register: brand headline
aspect: 16x9

One line on screen, and the promise inside it keeps upgrading. The sentence frame stays put, the reader keeps reading the same sentence, but the claim in the middle of it turns over a few times and then stops on the one we actually mean. Think of it as the same sentence being said three ways, with the last way being the real one. Critical: the line must not jump around while this happens. If the words on either side of the changing part shift position, the effect reads as broken layout rather than as a deliberate device. It should land settled, not mid-motion.

gold best: kinetic-type-swap
known wrong: version-plate-type, particle-text-dissolve, glyph-ring-assemble

version-plate-type sits in the bad list on purpose. It is the selection a run makes when it matches on the words 'text' and 'changes' without reading what the move actually does to the sentence.

brief 05

id: 05
duration: 2.8
register: product spatial
aspect: 16x9

Two things are stacked in space: the thing the viewer is looking at now, up close, and the thing we want them looking at instead, sitting further back. The beat is the handoff. Attention starts on the near one and ends on the far one, and it should feel like the eye refocusing rather than like a slide swapping out. It happens once and it does not come back. Sharpness, weight, and a little positional drift should all agree about where the viewer is supposed to be looking by the end. Short beat, so the handoff cannot dawdle.

gold best: depth-rack-focus, focus-rack
known wrong: z-punch-through, orbit-ring-camera

focus-rack racks exactly once over 0.7 seconds, which fits a 2.8 second beat. A run that rejects it for being too slow has not read the envelope.
